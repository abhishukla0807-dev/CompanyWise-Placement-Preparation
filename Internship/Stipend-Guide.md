## What is a Stipend

A stipend is a fixed monthly allowance paid by a company to an intern. It is not the same as a full salary. It is meant to cover basic daily expenses like food, transport, and accommodation during the internship period.

Stipends can be paid in different ways:

- Fixed monthly amount
- Project-based lump sum after completion
- Performance-based incentives on top of a base amount
- Non-monetary benefits like meals, travel allowance, or accommodation

## Stipend Ranges in India

These are realistic ranges based on 2025-2026 data across different tiers:

| Category | Monthly Stipend |
|----------|----------------|
| Unpaid | 0 |
| Low stipend (small startups, NGOs) | Rs. 2,000 to Rs. 8,000 |
| Standard (mid-size companies) | Rs. 8,000 to Rs. 20,000 |
| Good (funded startups, MNCs) | Rs. 20,000 to Rs. 40,000 |
| Premium (top tech companies) | Rs. 40,000 to Rs. 1,00,000+ |

> The average internship stipend in India ranges from Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 30,000 per month for most roles.

Metro cities like Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi, and Hyderabad tend to pay 10 to 20% higher than smaller towns, largely due to the cost of living difference.

## Factors That Affect Stipend

Your stipend is not random. These factors directly influence what you are offered:

- **Company size and funding** - Larger and better-funded companies pay significantly more
- **Location** - Metro cities pay higher due to cost of living
- **Role and domain** - SDE, Data Science, and AI roles typically pay more than non-technical ones
- **Your skills and profile** - A strong GitHub, competitive programming profile, or relevant projects can increase your offer
- **College reputation** - Some companies have tier-based stipend structures
- **Remote vs on-site** - Remote roles sometimes offer lower stipends since there is no commute cost
- **Duration** - Longer internships (3 to 6 months) sometimes come with higher monthly stipends

| Motivation and recognition | Higher | Can be lower |

In India, labor laws around unpaid internships are still evolving. There is no strict enforcement requiring a minimum stipend for student interns, but some universities and states do set basic requirements.

## Is an Unpaid Internship Worth It

This is one of the most debated questions among freshers. The honest answer is: it depends entirely on what you are actually getting in return.

**An unpaid internship may be worth it if:**
- The company has a strong brand name that will carry weight on your resume
- You are getting real project work and direct mentorship from experienced professionals
- The domain or role is exactly aligned with your career goals
- You have financial support and do not need an income right now
- The internship can realistically lead to a PPO or a strong referral

**An unpaid internship is likely not worth it if:**
- Your tasks are mostly clerical, administrative, or unrelated to your field
- There is no mentor, no guidance, and no structured learning
- The company is not known and the experience will not open meaningful doors
- You are taking on financial stress that affects your focus and well-being
- It runs longer than 3 months with no path to compensation

> Employers never ask if you were paid. They ask what you built and what you learned. The quality of the experience matters more than the paycheck. But that only holds if the experience is genuinely good.

## How to Negotiate Your Stipend

Negotiating as a fresher feels uncomfortable, but it is both acceptable and professional if done the right way.

**When you can negotiate:**
- You have competing offers from other companies
- Your skills, projects, or certifications clearly add value
- The company is a startup or mid-size firm (large MNCs often have fixed structures)
- You have been in the role for a few weeks and have demonstrated your contribution

**How to approach it:**
- Research the average stipend for your role, city, and company type before the conversation
- Express genuine enthusiasm for the role first, then bring up the stipend
- Base your ask on market data, not personal financial need
- Be specific about your value, mention projects, skills, or outcomes
- If they cannot increase the stipend, ask about non-monetary benefits like travel allowance or a PPO possibility

**What to avoid:**
- Negotiating too early, before you have even started
- Comparing yourself to friends at other companies in a public or aggressive way
- Saying "I really need the money" as your argument
- Negotiating just for the sake of it without a clear rationale

> It almost never hurts to ask. A respectful, well-researched ask rarely costs you the offer.

## Other Benefits to Look For

Stipend is not the only thing of value in an internship. Always evaluate the full picture:

- **Pre-Placement Offer (PPO)** - A conversion to full-time employment after the internship based on performance
- **Letter of Recommendation** - A strong LOR from a senior professional carries significant weight
- **Certificate** - Proof of completion that goes on your resume and LinkedIn
- **Mentorship** - Direct access to experienced engineers or managers who invest in your growth
- **Travel or meal allowance** - Some companies offer these in lieu of or alongside the stipend
- **Access to tools and courses** - Some companies provide paid course subscriptions or certifications during the internship
- **Networking** - Building relationships with people who can refer you for future roles

> A Rs. 10,000 stipend with a PPO, strong mentorship, and a well-known company name is often more valuable than a Rs. 25,000 stipend at a company with none of those.

---

## Red Flags to Watch Out For

Not every internship is worth your time. Watch for these warning signs:

- No clear role or description before joining
- All tasks are clerical, repetitive, or unrelated to your domain
- No assigned mentor or point of contact for learning
- Company asks you to pay a fee to "register" or access the internship
- Stipend is withheld until "after you meet targets" with no clear metrics
- No internship agreement or documentation provided
- Extended unpaid periods with vague promises of future payment
- Your work output is being used commercially with no acknowledgment or compensation

> If a company cannot define what you will learn and what you will do, that is a signal to ask harder questions before accepting.

---

## Final Thoughts

- Stipend matters, but it is one factor among many
- Freshers should prioritize learning quality, mentorship, and company credibility over a higher number
- Unpaid internships can be worth it if the experience is real and the company name adds value to your profile
- Negotiate when it makes sense, back your ask with research and demonstrated value
- Always get your offer documented and confirm your stipend, joining date, and duration in writing
- Update your resume, LinkedIn, and GitHub at the end of every internship regardless of the stipend

> Your first internship is an investment in your career. Be intentional about where you spend that time.
