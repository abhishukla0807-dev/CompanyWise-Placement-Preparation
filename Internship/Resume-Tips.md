## What is a Resume

A resume is a concise, professional summary of your education, skills, and experience. Its only job is to earn you an interview.

- It is not an autobiography
- It is not a list of everything you have ever done
- It is a curated, targeted document built for a specific role

> Tailor your resume for every role you apply to. A generic resume rarely works.

---

## Resume Structure

A well-structured resume follows a clear, consistent layout that a recruiter can scan in seconds.

**Recommended order:**

- Name and contact information
- Professional introduction or summary
- Work experience
- Education
- Internships (if applicable)
- Certifications (relevant only)
- Skills
- Hobbies or additional sections (freshers only, if needed)

Keep the layout clean. Use consistent spacing between each section. Do not crowd the page.

## Section-by-Section Breakdown

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Name and contact | First thing a recruiter sees |
| Introduction | Quick snapshot of who you are professionally |
| Experience | Your most important section |
| Education | Academic background |
| Certifications | Proof of relevant skills |
| Skills | What you bring to the role |
| Hobbies | Optional, for freshers with limited experience |

## Writing Your Introduction

The introduction sits at the top of your resume, right after your name and contact details. It is 2 to 3 lines that tell the recruiter who you are at a glance.

**What to include:**
- Your role or field
- Your overall level of experience (do not mention number of years)
- A key strength or area of expertise

**What to avoid:**
- Company names
- Specific years or numbers
- Generic filler phrases like "hard-working" without context

---

#### ✦ For Experienced Professionals

> Skilled backend engineer with expertise in distributed systems and API development. Experienced in building scalable solutions across fintech and SaaS environments.

#### ✦ For Freshers

> Recent computer science graduate with a strong foundation in Java and web development. Highly motivated software developer actively seeking opportunities to contribute and grow.

## Writing Your Experience

List your experience in reverse chronological order, most recent first.

**Each entry should include:**
- Company name
- Your job title or role, placed directly below the company name
- Duration on the right side of the page (e.g., Mar 2020 - Jun 2021)
- A short description of what you did and what you achieved

**What to write in the description:**
- Focus on what you contributed, not just what your responsibilities were
- Use numbers where possible to show impact
- Keep each bullet point to one line if possible, two at most

**Example:**

```
ABC Technologies
Backend Developer                                    Mar 2022 - Present

- Built and maintained REST APIs serving 50,000+ daily active users
- Reduced API response time by 30% through query optimization
- Collaborated with frontend teams to integrate real-time notification systems
```

> Write about what you did at the company. This is different from your introduction, which covers your overall professional profile.

## Skills Section

The skills section should reflect your genuine strengths, not a list of everything you have touched.

- Add only the skills you are confident in
- Include technical skills relevant to your job role
- Add soft skills like communication or research only if truly relevant
- Do not pad this section with basic tools everyone knows

**Example for a developer:**
- Java, Spring Boot, REST APIs
- MySQL, PostgreSQL
- Git, Docker basics
- Problem solving, technical communication

> Quality over quantity. A short, honest skills section is far stronger than a bloated one.

## Education, Internships and Certifications

### Education

- List your degree, institution name, and year of completion
- No need to add detailed descriptions
- For senior professionals, institution name and dates are sufficient

### Internships

- Format exactly like work experience
- Include company name, your role, duration, and a brief description
- Treat internships seriously, especially as a fresher

### Certifications

- Add only certifications relevant to the role you are applying for
- Add a one-line description if the certification needs context
- As a fresher, include certifications that demonstrate initiative and learning

## Formatting Guidelines

Good formatting makes your resume readable in seconds. Poor formatting loses you the interview before it starts.

**Font and size:**
- Use clean fonts like Arial, Calibri, or Helvetica
- Body text between 10 and 12 points
- Name and section headings up to 14 points

**Layout:**
- Margins between 1 and 1.5 inches
- Use bullet points to structure experience and skills
- Leave enough white space between sections
- Use bold or italics sparingly, only to highlight key details

**Length:**
- Fresher or early career: one page
- Experienced professionals: one to two pages, never more

**Design:**
- Choose light, simple templates
- Avoid dark backgrounds and overly decorative designs
- Canva has many clean, free templates worth exploring

**File format:**
- Always save and send as PDF
- Check that formatting looks correct after converting

## Action Verbs to Use

Start every bullet point with a strong action verb. Never begin with "I" or passive phrases.

| Category | Verbs |
|----------|-------|
| Leadership | Led, Directed, Managed, Coordinated, Spearheaded, Oversaw |
| Technical | Built, Developed, Engineered, Designed, Optimized, Implemented |
| Communication | Presented, Drafted, Collaborated, Negotiated, Reported |
| Research | Analyzed, Investigated, Evaluated, Identified, Examined |
| Achievement | Achieved, Improved, Reduced, Increased, Delivered, Launched |
| Teaching | Trained, Guided, Mentored, Facilitated, Coached |
| Organization | Streamlined, Structured, Planned, Executed, Monitored |

## For Freshers

If you are a fresher with limited work experience, your resume still needs to be strong and intentional.

**What to focus on:**
- A clear, motivated introduction that reflects your enthusiasm and direction
- Internships, even short ones, add significant value
- Academic projects with real outcomes
- Relevant certifications that show you are proactively learning
- Hobbies or activities that demonstrate discipline, creativity, or leadership

**Introduction examples for freshers:**
- Recent graduate looking for opportunities in software development
- Highly motivated frontend developer seeking entry-level opportunities
- Fresher with hands-on project experience in data science and Python

**What not to do:**
- Do not leave large empty spaces just to fill the page
- Do not add irrelevant certifications or hobbies for the sake of it
- Do not send the same resume for every application

---

## For Experienced Professionals

The more experience you have, the more selective you need to be.

- Lead with your strongest and most recent experience
- Remove roles that are no longer relevant to your direction
- Keep education brief, just institution and dates
- Focus on impact and measurable results in every bullet
- Remove hobbies unless directly relevant

> At this stage, every line on your resume should be earning its place.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| ✗ Mistake | Fix |
|-----------|-----|
| Spelling or grammar errors | Proofread using Grammarly before sending |
| Missing or wrong contact details | Double-check email and phone number |
| Passive language | Start every bullet with a strong action verb |
| Wrong length | One page for freshers, one to two for experienced |
| No measurable results | Add numbers, percentages, or outcomes |
| Same resume for every job | Tailor your resume for each role |
| Fancy or cluttered design | Use clean, light, simple templates |
| Too many skills listed | Include only your strongest and most relevant |
| Using personal pronouns | Never use "I", "my", or "we" |
| Irrelevant certifications | Include only what is relevant to the role |

---

## ✅ Final Checklist

Before sending your resume, go through this list:

- [ ] Name and contact details are correct and visible
- [ ] Introduction is tailored to the role
- [ ] Experience is in reverse chronological order
- [ ] Every bullet starts with an action verb
- [ ] At least one measurable result per role
- [ ] Skills section is concise and relevant
- [ ] Certifications are relevant to the job
- [ ] No spelling, grammar, or punctuation errors
- [ ] Formatting is clean, consistent, and well-spaced
- [ ] Saved and exported as PDF
- [ ] Resume is one to two pages, not more

> A well-built resume does not guarantee a job. But a poorly built one will cost you the interview.
