## How to Use This Guide

The US tech internship market operates differently from India or other markets:

- Timelines are far earlier — applications open 4 to 6 months before the internship starts
- University affiliation matters significantly — many top roles are routed through campus recruiting
- Visa eligibility filters much of the market for international students
- Platforms are not interchangeable — each serves a different hiring model

Use at least 3 to 4 platforms simultaneously. Apply early. Do not wait for the perfect listing.

---

## 🎓 Tier 1 — University-Integrated & Student-First Platforms

These platforms are built exclusively for students and are the primary channels US universities use to connect students with employers.

---

### [Handshake](https://joinhandshake.com/)

The most important internship platform for students studying in the US. Handshake is used by over 1,400 universities and connects more than 12 million students with 750,000+ employers.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Most US colleges use Handshake as their official career platform — many exclusive listings exist only here
- Employers on Handshake are specifically looking for students and early-career talent, not experienced professionals
- AI-powered profile matching surfaces relevant roles without constant manual searching
- Visa status filter saves international students from wasting time on ineligible roles
- Recruiters actively message students directly — a complete profile often brings opportunities to you

**✅ Best for:** Any student enrolled in a US university. This is your primary platform.

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | University-exclusive listings, visa filter, inbound recruiter messages, career fair integration |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Requires a US university email to access full features; internship postings declined 15%+ between 2023 and 2025 while applications surged |

**Tips:**
- Fill every section of your profile completely — a complete profile is five times more likely to receive a recruiter message
- Set job alerts immediately so you are notified the moment relevant roles are posted
- Follow companies you are targeting and attend their virtual events on the platform
- Apply as early as possible — September and January are peak recruiting months for larger employers

---

### [Simplify](https://simplify.jobs/)

A browser extension and internship database heavily used by US tech students to track, organize, and apply to hundreds of tech internships efficiently.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Aggregates thousands of tech internship listings from company career pages in one place
- Browser extension auto-fills application forms across multiple company websites, saving significant time
- Tracks application status across all companies in a single dashboard
- Curated tech-specific listings reduce noise significantly compared to general job boards
- Community-maintained lists (like Pitt CSC on GitHub) are often linked here for seasonal tracking

**✅ Best for:** CS students who want to apply to a high volume of tech internships efficiently without losing track

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | Auto-fill extension, centralized tracking, tech-focused curation, time efficiency |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Still requires tailored resumes per role; auto-fill does not replace customization |

**Tips:**
- Install the browser extension early in your job search cycle
- Use it alongside Handshake, not as a replacement
- Do not rely solely on auto-fill — customize your resume for competitive roles even if the extension handles the form

## 🚀 Tier 2 — Tech-Focused Job Boards & Startup Ecosystems

---

### [Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/) *(Formerly AngelList Talent)*

The go-to platform for startup internships in the US. Direct access to founders and early-stage companies, with full salary and equity transparency.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Startups on Wellfound often move faster than large companies — application to offer in days, not months
- Direct founder messaging is built into the platform
- Salary and funding stage are fully visible upfront, so there are no surprises
- Many YC-backed and venture-funded startups hire through Wellfound

**✅ Best for:** Students targeting funded startups, equity-based compensation, or faster hiring pipelines

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | Salary transparency, founder access, fast hiring cycles, startup depth |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Higher risk at early-stage startups; fewer large corporate roles |

---

### [Y Combinator Work at a Startup](https://www.workatastartup.com/)

The official job board for YC-backed startups. YC alumni companies include Airbnb, Dropbox, Reddit, Stripe, and thousands of others.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Every company listed has gone through YC's rigorous selection process — a signal of quality and seriousness
- Many YC startups use internships as direct pipelines to full-time offers
- YC runs a Summer Intern Batch matching process in fall and winter each year
- Roles are primarily SDE, data science, and product — all high-value technical tracks

**✅ Best for:** Students who want to work at high-growth, well-funded startups with real technical challenges

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | High-quality startups, fast decision cycles, pipeline to full-time offers |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Less structured than big-company internships; fewer formal support systems |

---

### [Pitt CSC Summer Internships List](https://github.com/pittcsc/Summer2025-Internships)

An open-source GitHub repository maintained by the Pitt Computer Science Club and contributors worldwide. Tracks which US tech companies are actively hiring interns each season.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Community-maintained and updated in near real-time during recruiting season
- Includes application status, deadline information, and direct links to company portals
- Covers companies ranging from FAANG to mid-size product companies to well-funded startups
- Free, open, and more current than most job boards during peak season

**✅ Best for:** CS students who want a live, curated tracker of every active tech internship in the US market

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | Real-time updates, community-driven, free, comprehensive coverage |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Requires you to apply directly on company websites; not a platform itself |

**Tips:**
- Bookmark this repository and check it weekly from August through February
- Star and watch the repo to receive GitHub notifications when new listings are added

---

### [Interstride](https://interstride.com/)

A career platform built specifically for international students studying in the US. Highlights employers with a proven history of sponsoring H-1B visas and hiring international candidates.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Filters employers by visa sponsorship history, saving international students from wasting applications
- Aggregates postings from multiple sources and tags international-friendly companies
- Provides additional career resources tailored to navigating the US job market as a foreign national

**✅ Best for:** International students on F-1 visa looking for CPT or OPT eligible roles

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | Visa-friendly employer filtering, international student focus, aggregated listings |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Interface is not as modern as other platforms; full feature access requires registration |

---

### [Untapped](https://untapped.io/) *(Formerly Canvas by JumpStart)*

A platform connecting candidates from underrepresented groups with top tech companies. Heavily skewed toward software engineering and data roles.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Companies actively recruit diverse candidates through Untapped rather than waiting for applications
- Profile showcases background, identity groups, and interests alongside resume — holistic candidate view
- Strong corporate partnerships with companies that have explicit diversity hiring goals

**✅ Best for:** Students from underrepresented groups including women in STEM, first-generation students, and minority communities in tech

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | Inbound corporate interest, diversity-focused, tech-heavy listings |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Limited to tech and some business roles; smaller listing volume than general boards |

## 🌐 Tier 3 — Broad Aggregators & Professional Networks

---

### [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/)

Essential in any market. In the US, LinkedIn is used heavily by both students and recruiters for tech internship sourcing.

**✦ Why it works:**
- Set highly specific alerts — "Software Engineering Intern 2026 Remote" — and receive instant notifications
- Direct message recruiters and engineering managers at target companies
- Alumni network at your university is one of the most underused resources for referrals
- Company pages show recent hires, team size, and often post internship announcements directly

| | |
|--|--|
| ✦ Strengths | Recruiter access, alumni networking, job alerts, brand visibility |
| ✗ Weaknesses | Ghost listings are common; requires an optimized, active profile to stand out |

---

### [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/)

A broad aggregator that pulls listings directly from company career pages. Useful for finding roles not listed on niche platforms.

## 🌏 For International Students — Visa Basics

If you are an Indian CS student studying in the US or applying from India, this section is critical.

**CPT (Curricular Practical Training)**
- Allows F-1 students to work off-campus as part of their curriculum
- Must be authorized by your university and directly related to your major
- Requires at least one full academic year of enrollment before you are eligible
- Authorization is per employer and per semester — apply through your DSO (Designated School Official)

**OPT (Optional Practical Training)**
- Allows F-1 students to work in the US for up to 12 months after graduation
- STEM OPT extension adds 24 more months for CS, engineering, and data science graduates
- Must apply at least 90 days before your intended start date
- OPT is the most common path for Indian CS students to convert an internship to a full-time offer

**J-1 Visa**
- A cultural exchange visa that allows structured internship participation
- Often used for students not enrolled in a US university who want to intern in the US through a sponsor organization

**Key rules:**
- Never intern in the US on a tourist visa — it is illegal and can result in deportation
- Always verify CPT or OPT authorization before accepting any offer
- Many companies explicitly state "no visa sponsorship" — use Interstride or Handshake's visa filter to avoid these listings

## 📅 Application Timeline for US Internships

| Period | What to Do |
|--------|-----------|
| August to September | Start tracking Pitt CSC list; set alerts on Handshake and LinkedIn |
| September to October | Apply to early-opening roles at large companies (Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon) |
| October to December | Peak application season — apply consistently across all platforms |
| January to February | Second wave of applications opens for mid-size companies and startups |
| March to April | Final opportunities; many roles fill by now |
| May to August | Summer internship season |

> Top US tech companies open applications for summer internships in August and September of the prior year. Applying in March for a summer role is almost always too late for FAANG-level positions.

## 🎯 Which Platform Should You Use

**If you are a student enrolled at a US university:**
- Handshake first, always
- Simplify for volume and tracking
- Pitt CSC GitHub list for real-time awareness

**If you are targeting funded startups:**
- Wellfound and Y Combinator Work at a Startup

**If you are an international student concerned about visa eligibility:**
- Interstride as your primary filter
- Handshake with visa filter enabled

**If you are from an underrepresented group:**
- Untapped alongside your standard applications

**If you are applying from India without US enrollment:**
- LinkedIn, Wellfound, and Interstride
- Target remote-first roles at US companies that have hired internationally before
- GSoC and MLH Fellowship remain your most accessible pathways

##  Common Mistakes on US Platforms ❌

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Applying in March for summer internships | Start in August or September for the following summer |
| Using only one platform | Use Handshake, Simplify, and LinkedIn at minimum |
| Incomplete Handshake profile | Fill every section — incomplete profiles are rarely surfaced |
| Ignoring visa eligibility before applying | Use Interstride or Handshake visa filter first |
| Not tracking applications | Use Simplify's dashboard or a spreadsheet |
| Sending generic applications | Customize resume for every role you care about |
| Overlooking the Pitt CSC GitHub list | Check it weekly during recruiting season |
| Not attending virtual career fairs on Handshake | These give direct recruiter access most students skip |
